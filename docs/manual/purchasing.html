Buy → Receive → Bill → Pay

Purchasing is one chain of documents. Each screen picks up where the previous one stopped, and each step updates stock, the books or cash on its own. Nobody re-types anything.

The chain at a glance

Five documents, each answering one question. The chips show what changes when you save it.

    master data
  2. Purchase order
    What did we order?
    nothing posted
  3. Receiving report
    What actually arrived?
    stock ↑cost updated
  4. Supplier bill
    What did they charge?
    we owe ↑input VAT
  5. Payment made
    What did we pay?
    cash ↓we owe ↓EWT for BIR

Each screen

In the order you would use them. The sixth screen, Payables, is a list that watches the other five.

1

Suppliers

Purchasing → Suppliers

The supplier's record: code, registered name, TIN, address, payment terms, whether they are VAT-registered, and their default withholding (EWT) rate.

  • These settings fill in every later document: terms set the bill's due date, VAT status decides if VAT is charged, the EWT rate is deducted when you pay.
  • The supplier page shows a statement of account: every bill and payment with a running balance, plus "We owe" and "Overdue".
  • Shortcut buttons: New PO, New bill, Pay bills.
2

Purchase orders

Purchasing → Purchase orders

What you asked the supplier for: products, quantities, unit cost (before VAT), the VAT type of each line, and the warehouse it should go to.

  • Saving a PO does not change stock or the books. It is a commitment, not a transaction.
  • Status moves by itself: Open → Partly received → Received.
  • If the rest is not coming, Close the order. If nothing arrived, Cancel it. Orders are never edited.
  • Print gives the PO to send to the supplier, with delivery address, VAT, total in words and a "supplier conforme" signature line.
3

Receiving reports

Open a purchase order → Receive goods

Record what physically arrived. One order can be received in several deliveries.

  • Stock goes up in the order's warehouse right away (it appears under Inventory → Movements).
  • The product's cost becomes the average of the stock you had and the stock that arrived.
  • The books record goods you owe for but have not been billed for yet ("Goods received not invoiced").
  • Each line shows Not billed until a supplier bill covers it.
  • Print gives the receiving report for the warehouse file: ordered vs received, with delivered / received / checked signatures.
Example: 10 drums on hand at ₱100 + 5 arrive at ₱130 → 15 drums at ₱110 each.
4

Supplier bills

Purchasing → Supplier bills → New

Enter the supplier's invoice when it arrives. Pick the supplier and branch, tick the received goods it covers, and add any expense lines (freight, repairs, rent).

  • Records input VAT and the amount you now owe the supplier. The due date comes from the supplier's terms.
  • The supplier's invoice number is required and cannot be entered twice.
  • A bill with no payments can be voided; its goods can then be billed again.
  • Opening payables: enter a bill with an expense line against Opening balance equity.
  • Print voucher gives the accounts payable voucher, with the accounting entry and prepared / checked / approved lines.
5

Payments made

Purchasing → Payments made → New

Pick a supplier, then how much to pay on each open bill. One payment can cover several bills of one branch, and part-payments are fine.

  • Withholding tax is worked out for you and kept for the BIR.
  • Cash leaves the cash book, and what you owe goes down by the full amount (cash + withholding).
  • Print voucher gives the check (or disbursement) voucher: bills paid, withholding, amount in words, the entry, and a "received payment by" line.
Example: bill of ₱3,472 (₱3,100 + ₱372 VAT) at 1% EWT → ₱31 withheld, ₱3,441 paid by check.
6

Payables

Purchasing → Payables

Every unpaid bill, oldest due date first, with days overdue. Your "who do we pay this week" list. Filter by age, branch or supplier; export to Excel.

A typical week

The same chain, as it plays out in a real branch.

Order, receive, bill, pay

  1. Stock is low, so you create a purchase order to ABC Trading for 100 drums.
  2. 60 drums arrive. Open the PO and receive 60. The PO shows "Partly received".
  3. ABC's invoice comes in. Create a bill, tick the 60 drums and add the delivery fee.
  4. On Friday check Payables, then record the payment with the check number.
  5. The other 40 drums are not coming, so close the PO.

Pay a bill for a service (no goods)

  1. Supplier bills → New, choose the supplier and branch.
  2. Add an expense line: account (e.g. Rent and utilities), description, amount before VAT.
  3. Check the withholding rate (e.g. 5% for rent) and post.
  4. Pay it from the bill page with "Pay this bill".

Ideas to make it stronger

"Planned" items are already in the roadmap. "New" items are suggestions.

Purchase requests New

Staff ask for items; purchasing turns approved requests into POs.

PO approval by amount Planned

Orders over a limit wait for a manager.

Form 2307 printing Planned

Print the withholding certificate straight from a payment.

Reorder suggestions New

Products below their reorder level become a draft PO in one click.

Supplier price history New

Last price paid per product, with a warning when a PO price jumps.

Purchase returns New

Send damaged goods back; stock and what you owe both go down.

Landed cost New

Spread freight and duties into product cost instead of booking them as an expense.
